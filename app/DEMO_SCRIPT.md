# Live Slides — TED-style demo

This is a continuous three-and-a-half-minute talk. Everything in quotation marks is spoken to the audience. Italic text describes the expected visual and is never read aloud.

The talk begins with one short human moment, moves quickly into evidence, and reveals Live Slides only at the end. The presenter never asks the screen to create anything.

## 0:00–0:30 — A human hook

“At eleven o’clock one Thursday night, I walked past an almost empty campus basketball court.”

*A basketball photo appears.*

“Six students were playing. None of them had known each other an hour earlier.”

“That game made me wonder whether belonging is something we can design, rather than something we simply hope will happen.”

## 0:30–1:10 — Define the problem

“We asked two hundred students what kept them away from campus activities.”

“Forty-six percent said they had nobody to go with. Thirty-two percent did not know where to begin. Twenty percent felt they were not good enough to join.”

*A three-part reasons-for-not-joining chart appears.*

“I need to correct the first number. It was forty-eight percent, not forty-six.”

*Only Nobody to go with changes from 46% to 48%.*

“That small correction matters, because nearly half the students were not rejecting community. They were waiting for an invitation.”

## 1:10–1:55 — Measure an experiment

“So we ran a five-week experiment built around open pickup games.”

“Attendance started with twelve students in week one, grew to twenty-nine in week two, fifty-one in week three, and seventy-eight in week four.”

*A four-point attendance chart appears beside the first chart.*

“By week five, one hundred three students were participating.”

*Week 5 is added to the same attendance chart.*

“The first week was a private organizer test, so the public launch data really begins in week two.”

*Week 1 is removed; Weeks 2 through 5 remain.*

“From twenty-nine people to one hundred three in four public weeks: the demand had been there all along.”

## 1:55–2:35 — Compare what worked

“But turnout is not the same as belonging, so we measured how many people came back.”

“A one-time drop-in event retained twenty-eight percent of participants. A recurring team retained sixty-four percent. A student-led group retained eighty-one percent.”

*A retention comparison chart appears without replacing attendance.*

“Side by side, participation and retention tell different parts of the same story: access brings people in, but ownership brings them back.”

*The charts arrange for comparison.*

“The recurring-team result was from an early export. The final value was sixty-seven percent.”

*Only Recurring team changes from 64% to 67%.*

“The attendance sequence remains twenty-nine, fifty-one, seventy-eight, and one hundred three.”

*The earlier attendance chart remains unchanged rather than being redrawn.*

## 2:35–3:05 — Explain the mechanism

“The numbers led us to a simple three-step model.”

“First, make the first step obvious. Then, give every newcomer one immediate connection. Finally, hand the group enough ownership to continue without you.”

*A three-step process diagram appears.*

“There is one step missing between connection and ownership: give people a small role before asking for a large commitment.”

*A Small role step is inserted into the existing diagram.*

“So the pattern is visibility, connection, a small role, and then ownership.”

*The existing diagram remains; no duplicate is created.*

## 3:05–3:30 — Reveal Live Slides

“You may have noticed that this presentation had no prepared deck.”

“The photograph followed the story. The charts followed the evidence. The diagram followed the idea. Even the corrections changed what was already here.”

*No visual change.*

“I never stopped speaking to tell a screen what to draw.”

“That is Live Slides: a visual canvas that listens closely enough to keep up with the way people actually think.”

*End the session after the final line.*

## Natural recovery lines

If a visual misses, fold one clarification into the talk and continue.

- Survey: “The full breakdown was forty-eight percent alone, thirty-two percent unsure where to begin, and twenty percent worried about ability.”
- Attendance: “The weekly public attendance was twenty-nine, fifty-one, seventy-eight, and one hundred three.”
- Retention: “Retention was twenty-eight percent for drop-ins, sixty-seven percent for recurring teams, and eighty-one percent for student-led groups.”
- Framework: “The four stages are visibility, connection, a small role, and ownership.”
- Section change: “That explains the result. Now I want to explain why it worked.”

Keep talking through render time. Use one precise clarification instead of immediately repeating an entire paragraph.
